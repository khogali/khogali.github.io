We get a version of the same note most weeks. It usually starts with an embarrassed disclaimer along the lines of "I am not a tech person," and then asks a perfectly good question.

“My son told me to stop using the Wi-Fi at the café. He couldn't really explain why. Is it actually a problem, or is he being dramatic?”

Who can actually see what you do

He is not being dramatic, but the reason is more boring than the movies suggest. Nobody needs to “hack” you. When your phone joins a network, the people who run that network can see which sites and apps it talks to. On a café's Wi-Fi that is the café, or whoever set up the router for them. At home it is your internet provider.

In the United States, providers have been allowed to collect and sell that browsing history since 2017, when the rules that would have stopped them were repealed. That is not a rumour; it was a vote in Congress. Most people never read about it, and it is entirely legal.

  • The Wi-Fi you join can see where your phone goes online
  • Your home provider can keep that history and sell it on
  • None of this requires you to click anything wrong

That last point is the one that lands. The usual advice is “be careful,” and most people over 50 already are. This is not a carefulness problem. The watching happens whether you are careful or not.

What most advice gets wrong

Most of it is about passwords and scams. That advice is good, and it does nothing about this. A strong password protects your account; it does not stop the network from seeing which bank's website you just opened.

The people who have actually changed the pattern rarely talk about being more careful. They talk about one setting that makes the watching pointless, so they can stop thinking about it.

The setting

It is called a VPN, which is an unfortunate name for a simple idea. When it is on, everything your phone sends is sealed before it leaves the phone. The café, the hotel, the airport, your provider: they can see that your phone is online and nothing else. Where you go is your business again.

You turn it on once. It stays on. There is nothing to remember, which is the whole point; it works on the days you are tired and not paying attention, which is when the other advice fails.
