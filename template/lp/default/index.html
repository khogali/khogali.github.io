Why a patch, in one paragraph

Berberine is a plant compound that has been studied for a long time in metabolic and weight-management contexts. Its well-known problem is not whether it does anything. It is that swallowing it works poorly: oral berberine has famously low absorption and is hard on the stomach, which is why people who try the capsules tend to quit them. A skin patch bypasses the gut and releases gradually over hours instead of all at once. That is the whole argument, and it is at least a coherent one.

What people say they like about it
  • Nothing to swallow, nothing to remember mid-afternoon
  • Easier on the stomach than the capsule version
  • One patch in the morning; it is simply already running by three o'clock

Why three o'clock

If your willpower is fine all morning and collapses somewhere between two and four, that is not a character problem. It is the most common pattern readers describe to us, phrased almost identically every time.

“I am fine until mid-afternoon. Genuinely fine. Then somewhere around three I am standing in the kitchen eating something I did not decide to eat.”

The people who changed it rarely say they decided harder. They say they put something in place that did not need a decision, so it worked on the tired days too. That is the appeal of the patch: it asks nothing of you in the hour you have the least left to give.
