Every decision in this plan is one of these three numbers moving. Nothing else is a KPI.

1. Overview

Objective. By 2 October, one offer with a measured cost per sale under its payout on at least 100 Facebook Feed clicks and a full week of data, so that scaling is a decision rather than a hope. Secondary: a second offer live in parallel with its own tracking, and an email-capture step tested on the winning page.

Where it stands on day one. Day 1 spent $32.78 for 128 clicks and 2 click-outs. Day 2 spent $9.70 for 7 clicks after a payment stall. Those numbers are not a verdict on the offer; they are the Reels placement (105 clicks, zero reached the offer) and a page that hid the product three screens down. Both were fixed on 3 September. The campaign proper starts with clean inputs.

2. Audience

Primary. US adults 45 to 65, mobile, browsing the Facebook Feed. Meta's own breakdown put 74% of day-one spend on 65+ and 65+ women alone at 64%; they loaded the page 89% of the time. They are not tapping by accident. They arrive, and the page has to hold them.

What they have in common across both offers. They have tried the obvious advice. They distrust hype and respond to being told the truth about the next page. They read, if the first screen gives them a reason to.

3. Key messages

Core. Here is the thing, here is exactly how it works, here is what it does not do, and the next page is a sales page; we said so.

Purisaki

  • It is a patch, not another capsule. Named in the first sentence. Proof: the page now says so at 426px.
  • Berberine is poorly absorbed and hard on the stomach when swallowed; a patch bypasses the gut and releases over hours. Proof: mechanism language only, attributed to the company.
  • It asks nothing of you at three o'clock. Proof: the reader quote the page already uses.
  • Rails: no numbers, no before/after, no body imagery, no institution name-drops.

Private Internet Access

  • Whoever runs the network you join can see where your phone goes; in the US your provider may sell that history. Proof: the 2017 repeal is public record.
  • Password advice does not cover this. A VPN seals the traffic before it leaves the phone.
  • This one's no-logs claim has been tested in court and audited. Proof: attributed, checkable, not asserted.
  • Rails: no fear framing, no anonymity claims, honest about scams and phishing being out of scope.

9. Risks

Feed CPC stays above $1. Feed impressions are expensive and these creatives earned few clicks on day two. Mitigation: refresh creatives to match the new headline before touching budget; if CPC is still above $1 at Gate 1, the ads are the problem, not the page.

The account is three days old and has already tripped two identity checks and one payment stall. Mitigation: no edits to the ad set from the UI (the deprecated location field is gone, but edits still reset learning); billing checked before any diagnosis of "delivery dropped".

Kape or Orbio approval lags or forbids paid social. Mitigation: Proton is the fallback with paid platforms explicitly allowed; the privacy page needs only the offer link changed.
